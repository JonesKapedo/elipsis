# Competitive Edge and Customer Journey

Version 1.0, 22 October 2026.

## Market read

The logistics software market is crowded at the point of execution. Samsara, Motive, Geotab and Fleetio compete on telematics, fleet operations and maintenance. project44, Descartes and Manhattan compete on visibility, routing and supply-chain execution. SafetyCulture competes on frontline inspections and checklists. KPMG Atlas and similar advisory tools compete on maturity diagnostics.

Elipsis should not compete on sensor count, load boards or dispatch. Those markets reward capital, hardware integration and enterprise sales. Elipsis wins by owning the decision layer between a diagnostic and an operating cadence.

Observed strengths to learn from:

- Samsara and Motive make the operational data visible every day, not once a year.
- project44 sells a network and measurable visibility outcomes.
- Fleetio wins through workflow depth and maintenance ownership.
- SafetyCulture makes frontline adoption simple and mobile-first.
- KPMG Atlas gives an assessment credibility through a recognised methodology.

Observed gaps:

- Most tools show data but do not turn it into an owned management decision.
- Diagnostic tools stop at a score and a PDF.
- Enterprise platforms are expensive and slow to deploy for mid-market African operators.
- Global benchmarks often ignore local constraints such as fuel volatility, informal transport, foreign exchange and fragmented data.
- Support and trust are treated as cost centres, not differentiators.

## Elipsis position

**The operating system for turning a logistics assessment into measurable action.**

The promise is not "another dashboard". It is a closed loop:

1. Assess the operation in plain language.
2. Quantify the cost of the constraint.
3. Agree a small set of owned actions.
4. Run the weekly operating rhythm.
5. Prove movement with a repeat assessment and an audit trail.

## Defensible advantages

1. **Closed-loop workspace.** Assessment, costed constraint, plan, decisions, notes and team discussion live in one private company space. Competitors typically stop at reporting.
2. **Local context with global standards.** Emerging-market operating assumptions are explicit, while service delivery, security and accessibility follow global norms.
3. **Transparent economics.** The assessment shows assumptions and the cost of inaction. Premium is priced in USD at a level mid-market operators can approve without a procurement cycle.
4. **Service as product.** Published SLA, status, ticket transparency, health scoring and quarterly reviews make the customer relationship legible.
5. **Low-friction adoption.** A team can start with one assessment and invite managers without an implementation project.
6. **Trust by default.** Versioned legal terms, data export/erasure, DPA, subprocessors and security disclosure are available before a customer asks.

## Customer journey

### 1. Discover

Public proof: sample report, methodology, use cases, pricing, security, trust centre, status and help. The visitor should be able to answer "what will I get, what does it cost, and can I trust this vendor?" without a sales call.

### 2. Start

Signup with explicit terms acceptance. Immediate setup of company profile and first assessment. The user sees a seven-step activation path and one next action, not a feature tour.

### 3. First value

Complete the assessment. Receive a readiness index, confidence level, costed constraint and a 90-day plan. The result page must make the next decision obvious.

### 4. Team adoption

Invite a manager or supervisor. Assign actions. Capture a decision. Use the weekly desk and brief. The company health score reflects whether this is actually happening.

### 5. Operate

Weekly rhythm: desk, huddle, decisions, notes, actions. Repeat assessment after 90 days. The product becomes the operating record, not a one-off report.

### 6. Support and success

In-app support with a reference and SLA. Status and incident communication. CSAT after resolution. NPS quarterly. At-risk accounts get proactive outreach.

### 7. Renew and expand

Quarterly review with measured movement. Transparent renewal date and price. Expansion through more departments, assessments and team members, not through lock-in.

## Metrics that matter

- Visitor to signup conversion.
- Signup to first completed assessment within 14 days.
- First assessment to first owned action within 7 days.
- Team invitation rate and second-user activation.
- 90-day repeat assessment rate.
- Paid conversion and renewal.
- CSAT, NPS and CES.
- Health score distribution and at-risk accounts.
- Support SLA attainment and incident count.
- Data-request and legal-acceptance completeness.

## 90-day priorities

1. Ship the service-delivery layer: status, support, SLA, feedback, trust, help, data rights.
2. Instrument activation and health so the team can act before renewal risk.
3. Publish the peer-informed standard and use it in sales conversations.
4. Run weekly service reviews and monthly customer reviews.
5. Remove the largest trust gaps: automatic invite delivery, production auth verification, live USD payment verification and accessibility testing.

## What not to do

- Do not add telematics, dispatch or a load board. Those are different products with different economics.
- Do not add sidebar items for every service page. Use the trust centre, footer, settings and contextual links.
- Do not claim certifications or benchmarks that are not evidenced.
- Do not hide service failures behind marketing language.
- Do not optimise for feature count over activation and repeat use.
