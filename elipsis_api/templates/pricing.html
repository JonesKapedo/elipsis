Why this pricing holds up

The fee is tied to the decision you are making, not to the number of logins. Here is the arithmetic a finance lead should apply before buying.

01

One consultant day, not fourteen weeks

A credible operations review usually costs more than the Pilot. The Pilot gives you the same baseline, a costed constraint, and a plan you own — in one working session.

02

Less than an hour of labour per employee

For a 100-person operator, Operator costs about $5 per employee per month. If the work removes one hour of avoidable rework per employee per month, the plan pays for itself.

03

The cost of one unresolved constraint

One repeatedly delayed dispatch, one idle vehicle, or one late invoice run usually costs more than the annual plan. The diagnostic tells you which one is actually happening.

04

No lock-in

Start free. Buy the Pilot only after you have seen your own numbers. Leave before renewal and keep every report and export. Pricing should survive that test.

Questions buyers actually ask

Why not charge per user?

Because the value comes from one shared operating picture, not from restricting access. Seats are generous on purpose; the plan scales with operating units.

We are small. Is $490 a month realistic?

If the business is below 25 staff, start with the free diagnostic or the Pilot. Move to Operator only when you are running recurring reviews and need the ledger.

What if the diagnosis finds little?

Then you have saved the cost of a consulting engagement. The free diagnostic is designed to answer that question before you pay.

Do you work outside your first market?

Yes. The framework is operating-model based, not country specific. We invoice in local currency where required and support English delivery today.
